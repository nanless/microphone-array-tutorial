## Chapter 1: From Auditory Cues to Verifiable Array Tasks

Corresponding to [Chapter 1](../../../../chapters_en/01_problem-definition.md). This section distinguishes three kinds of evidence: propagation delays predicted by head-geometry models, discrimination thresholds measured in psychoacoustic experiments, and numerical estimates computed from audio recordings by software. Although the three inform one another conceptually, their inputs, outputs, and error definitions are distinct.

### Interaural Time Difference, Level Difference, and Direction-Dependent Spectra

Interaural Time Difference (ITD) characterizes the relative arrival timing between the two ear signals, whereas Interaural Level Difference (ILD) characterizes the relative sound level at a given frequency or within a specified frequency band. After propagating around the head, pinnae, and torso, an acoustic wave also acquires direction-varying spectral peaks and notches, which are most accurately termed direction-dependent spectral cues.

Under the main text's "right minus left" convention, when both ear response magnitudes at a given frequency are nonzero, `ILD = 20 log10(|H_R|/|H_L|)` in dB. If the left and right magnitudes are `1` and `0.5`, the ILD is approximately −6.02 dB; swapping the two ears yields +6.02 dB. Multiplying both ears simultaneously by the same nonzero gain leaves this ratio unchanged. This example illustrates a pure magnitude-ratio calculation—not independent per-ear normalization, nor complete head-related filtering.

A Head-Related Transfer Function (HRTF) is the complex frequency response at each ear, whose time-domain counterpart is the Head-Related Impulse Response (HRIR). Because a pair of HRTFs simultaneously captures both magnitude and phase at the left and right ears, it can be used to analyze ILD, interaural delay, and spectral coloration; it is not an unrelated "third sensor" separate from ITD and ILD. In the pinned revision of SAF, `HRIRs2HRTFs` transforms left and right HRIRs into the frequency domain, while `estimateITDs` extracts a scalar interaural delay from the same HRIR pair—directly illustrating how summary cues derive from full responses. [SAF header and array dimensions](https://github.com/leomccormack/Spatial_Audio_Framework/blob/18fd5aba46e20787b51f28f7197a68506c965c07/framework/modules/saf_hrir/saf_hrir.h)

Direction-dependent spectral shape cannot be read unconditionally out of an unknown single recording either. Because the spectrum observed at the ear reflects the product of the source spectrum and the directional propagation response, attributing spectral differences to direction requires known excitation, a reference measurement, or explicit statistical assumptions. For example, a single pure tone cannot reveal broadband pinna notch locations, and multiplying left and right channels by frequency-independent constants alters broadband level without simulating frequency-selective pinna filtering.

Machine microphone arrays obey the same linear observation structure, yet their physical transfer responses differ. Two ideal omnidirectional microphones in an unbaffled free field exhibit neither acoustic head shadowing nor pinna spectral notches. When collecting multichannel responses on a smart-speaker enclosure, robot head, or headset, one must record the device geometry, channel ordering, calibration gains, and measurement directions to construct that specific device's array manifold; an individual human HRTF must never be substituted directly as the steering vector of an arbitrary microphone array.

### What Geometric Delay, Psychoacoustic Thresholds, and Digital Sampling Each Answer

The Woodworth worked example in Chapter 1 uses head radius 0.0875 m and sound speed 343 m/s, applying the rigid-sphere ray-path approximation from straight ahead to the lateral pole. Recomputing from that chapter's formula gives an interaural delay of 8.9045 μs at 1° off median and 655.8154 μs at 90° lateral. Extra decimal digits are retained here strictly for intermediate verification, while the figure rounds them to approximately 9 μs and 656 μs. These numbers are geometric-model predictions—neither population human averages nor psychoacoustic just-noticeable differences.

In a 16 kHz digital signal, one sampling period is 62.5 μs. Thus those two delays correspond to approximately 0.1425 and 10.4930 sample periods, respectively. Non-integer sample delays pose no contradiction: physical acoustic propagation is continuous in time, whereas the sampling interval merely defines the discrete recording grid. Whether fractional-sample delays can be accurately estimated from bandlimited observations further depends on signal bandwidth, SNR, and model mismatch; merely shrinking the integer sampling interval does not by itself prove higher localization accuracy.

If the same interaural spacing is replaced by two unbaffled free-field sensors without a head, the maximum lateral delay follows the straight-line path `0.175 / 343` s, or approximately 510.2041 μs. The two values differ because their underlying propagation models differ. A two-channel audio file synthesized with a pure 656 μs delay can illustrate lateralization via time difference, yet without head-related filtering it must not be described as a complete simulation of a real binaural recording. [Model-assumption analysis: Aaronson & Hartmann, JASA 2014](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.4861243)

Brughera, Dunai, and Hartmann used equal-level headphone pure tones with synchronized onset/offset envelopes, asking listeners to discriminate left–right shifts between two presentation intervals. Figure 1 of their paper compares four listeners: the two most sensitive listeners still achieved converged thresholds at 1400 Hz, whereas no listener converged at 1450 Hz. Crucially, the paper's threshold quantity is the inter-interval `ΔITD`: for instance, a 10 μs right-leading interval followed by a 10 μs left-leading interval represents a 20 μs comparison cue. This quantity must not be misquoted as single-trial azimuth estimation error, nor extrapolated directly to broadband speech or high-frequency modulated envelopes. [Published text archived in the co-author's UPV repository: §II.A, p. 2840; §II.B and Fig. 1, p. 2841; §II.C.4, p. 2843](https://riunet.upv.es/server/api/core/bitstreams/f65b9337-1b1e-4013-9020-8338ac77ad6b/content)

All-zero inputs and strong secondary reflection peaks merit dedicated test cases because returning a finite floating-point delay does not by itself prove reliable acoustic source evidence.

### SOFA Data Interfaces and Inclusion Boundaries for New Assets

The Spatially Oriented Format for Acoustics (SOFA) standardizes storage of HRTFs as well as binaural or spatial room impulse responses. Format compatibility guarantees only how container arrays are organized; users must still inspect coordinate conventions, sampling rates, receiver ordering, delay fields, and dataset licenses inside each individual `.sofa` file. [SOFA project overview and specification portal](https://www.sofacoustics.org/mediawiki/index.php/Main_Page)

To read SOFA containers and query or interpolate left/right filters by direction, inspect the maintainer's official C library [libmysofa](https://github.com/hoene/libmysofa), linked directly from SOFA's [Software and APIs](https://www.sofacoustics.org/mediawiki/index.php/Software_and_APIs) page. The upstream loudness-normalization audit report was executed on 2026-10-01 UTC (following the 2026-09-30 literature review); that audit compiled the unmodified `loudness.c` and `tools.c` sources directly in a temporary directory against synthetic C structs, without downloading the SOFA measurement files under `share/` and `tests/` or running the full file-reading and rendering pipeline.

Separate delay fields require careful unit auditing. Although the pinned README documents delays returned by the float API as seconds and those returned by the `short` API as samples, static source inspection reveals a mismatch against the SOFA standard: SOFA defines `Data.Delay` in samples; `reader.c` loads that array verbatim, `interpolate.c` copies or averages it, and the float API (`easy.c`) returns that interpolated number directly without dividing by sampling rate. During resampling, `resample.c` scales `Data.Delay` by the ratio of new to old sampling rates (consistent with sample units), whereas the `short` API multiplies the interpolated value again by the sampling rate.

Consequently, engineers must neither trust the README's "seconds" annotation blindly nor ignore separate delay fields. Here we document a static source inconsistency in the pinned commit without having executed multi-rate `.sofa` files containing nonzero `Data.Delay`, so we make no claim regarding end-to-end rendering correctness. [SOFA GeneralFIR delay specification](https://www.sofaconventions.org/mediawiki/index.php/GeneralFIR); [pinned easy.c interface](https://github.com/hoene/libmysofa/blob/6cc5b15a73e9bd97810d03767082edda7f315881/src/hrtf/easy.c); [pinned resample.c implementation](https://github.com/hoene/libmysofa/blob/6cc5b15a73e9bd97810d03767082edda7f315881/src/hrtf/resample.c).

**Directional queries also require coordinate conversion.** In our main text's horizontal-plane convention, 0° points straight ahead and positive azimuth rotates clockwise toward the right ear; by contrast, pinned `libmysofa` adopts `+X` forward, `+Y` left, `+Z` up, with azimuth increasing counterclockwise. When listener view and up vectors align with the canonical horizontal frame under this spherical convention, the SOFA input azimuth equals the negative of our textbook azimuth: 30° to the right in our text corresponds to −30° (or 330°) in SOFA. Because our loudness probe queried only 0°, it could not detect a left–right mirror flip. Before querying any `.sofa` file, always inspect its coordinate type, `ListenerView`, `ListenerUp`, and receiver ordering; a simple sign flip does not hold if the coordinate frame has been rotated. [Pinned coordinate convention in README](https://github.com/hoene/libmysofa/blob/6cc5b15a73e9bd97810d03767082edda7f315881/README.md)

**Upstream interpolation controls on shared delays (executed 2026-10-04).** Our [standalone interpolation audit](../../ch01/examples/audit_libmysofa_interpolation.py) compiles the complete unmodified `interpolate.c` and `tools.c` files, feeding a two-position synthetic structure `(-1,0,0)` and `(1,0,0)` with explicit nearest-neighbor indices. This controlled probe bypasses HDF5/SOFA parsing, format checks, and K-D tree neighbor search.

The root cause lies in the shared-delay (`1×R`) branch of pinned `interpolate.c`: it initializes the accumulator with the nearest neighbor's delay weighted by inverse distance, omits shared delays inside the neighbor-accumulation loop (which only accumulates `M×R` per-direction delays), and yet divides both layouts by total weight `weight` at the end. At the equidistant midpoint where both weights equal 1, the numerator retains a single `[8,16]` term while dividing by 2. ## Foundational Models, Statistical Estimation, and Calibration

### 1. Far-Field and Near-Field Steering Vectors

Corresponding to main text §§2.1–2.3 and §2.7. Acoustic propagation models take sensor coordinates, source position or direction, physical frequency, and sound speed as inputs, producing each channel's complex response relative to a shared reference. Sensor coordinates, audio channel indices, and calibration gains in code must align one-to-one: entering centimeters where meters are expected corrupts propagation phase in a way no amount of diagonal loading can repair.

#### 1.1 Distinguishing Propagation Responses, Normalized Manifolds, and Beamforming Weights

Under the free-field point-source model with source-to-microphone distance $r_m>0$ and forward Fourier convention $e^{-j\omega t}$, the raw acoustic transfer response is proportional to $e^{-j2\pi f r_m/c}/r_m$. Factoring out sensor 1 as the common reference yields the relative response $(r_1/r_m)e^{-j2\pi f(r_m-r_1)/c}$ at channel $m$, whose dimensionless amplitude ratio $r_1/r_m$ equals 1 at channel 1; changing the reference sensor alters the mathematical parameterization without altering physical microphone recordings.

The far-field plane-wave approximation replaces path-length differences by inner-product coordinate projections and sets amplitude ratios $r_1/r_m \to 1$. Comparing absolute phases between near-field and far-field formulas without removing common propagation distance conflates bulk travel phase with array-aperture wavefront curvature. A clean verification sweeps source range outward along a fixed ray while separately tracking relative TDOA error, amplitude-ratio deviation, and reference-aligned phase error—three distinct error metrics.

In pinned `pyroomacoustics` 0.10.0, `doa/doa.py::ModeVector` cannot stand in for the complete spherical-wave response above: its `near` branch computes Euclidean distances yet constructs only unit-magnitude complex exponentials `exp(+j*omega*dist/c)` without $1/r$ spherical spreading attenuation, using internal sign and reference conventions. Moreover, as detailed in §8 below, the upper-level `DOA` constructor fails to forward `mode` into `ModeVector`; merely seeing `mode='near'` in a function signature does not prove that the near-field call chain is active. [Pinned ModeVector source](https://github.com/LCAV/pyroomacoustics/blob/0dd39f2614b7fc44b2cc63dbe7d60f4641068890/pyroomacoustics/doa/doa.py).

Acoular exposes another hierarchy of objects that must be kept distinct. At our pinned commit, `SteeringVector.transfer(f)` returns the propagation transfer matrix, where `fastFuncs.py::_transferCoreFunc` computes distance ratios relative to a reference distance alongside negative-exponential phases. Only afterward does `steer_vector(f)` convert that transfer matrix into beamforming weights according to `steer_type`. Because `classic`, `inverse`, `true level`, and `true location` apply four distinct amplitude normalizations, none of them should be blindly equated to our textbook's unit-reference relative steering vector. [Acoular steering and weight API](https://github.com/acoular/acoular/blob/13d3d7df74ac1a8135c7ec71da098cbbc03d8652/acoular/fbeamform.py); [propagation core](https://github.com/acoular/acoular/blob/13d3d7df74ac1a8135c7ec71da098cbbc03d8652/acoular/fastFuncs.py).

#### 1.2 Speed-of-Sound Parameters Must Match the Actual Implementation

Our main text adopts $c=343$ m/s as a standard room-temperature pedagogical constant. #### 1.3 From Ideal Arrays to Physical Devices

Real hardware channels additionally include transducer sensitivity, enclosure diffraction, analog preamplifier response, and anti-aliasing/decimation filters. Denoting channel $m$'s directional electro-acoustic response by $G_m(f,\Omega)$, the true relative array manifold incorporates $(G_m(f,\Omega)/G_1(f,\Omega))$ alongside geometric delay—not path-length differences alone. Direction-dependent baffle scattering cannot be equalized by a single scalar per channel, nor can sampling-rate drift across unsynchronized devices be permanently corrected by a static phase offset.

Acoular's `Calib.result()` multiplies each data block channel-by-channel by user-supplied calibration factors in `Pa/unit`; for frequency-domain inputs, channel-by-frequency factors are flattened into a 1-D vector of length `M*F` and broadcast row-wise across blocks rather than passed as a 2-D matrix. Crucially, this interface merely applies pre-measured factors—it neither measures transducer sensitivity automatically nor infers sensor coordinates or clock skew, and its XML loader ingests real-valued scalars rather than full complex frequency responses. ### 2. Image-Source and Ray-Tracing Room Simulation

Corresponding to main text §2.4. In `pyroomacoustics`, `room.py` and `libroom_src/` provide the Python scene graph and C++ geometric simulation engine, respectively. The image-source method (ISM) enumerates discrete specular reflection paths, while ray tracing models late diffuse energy transport with optional surface scattering. Neither geometric-acoustics model automatically captures edge diffraction, structural vibration flanking, loudspeaker nonlinearity, or ADC clipping. [Official room documentation](https://pyroomacoustics.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pyroomacoustics.room.html).

#### 2.1 From Target Reverberation Time to Simulator Inputs

`acoustics.py::inverse_sabine(rt60, room_dim, c)` returns the mean **energy absorption coefficient** alongside a recommended maximum image-source order. For a 3-D shoebox room, it inverts Sabine's formula from room volume, surface area, and sound speed, estimating maximum reflection order from specular travel radius. Importantly, the input `rt60` is a design target—not an empirical $T_{60}$ measured from the resulting synthesized RIR. [Pinned inverse_sabine source](https://github.com/LCAV/pyroomacoustics/blob/0dd39f2614b7fc44b2cc63dbe7d60f4641068890/pyroomacoustics/acoustics.py).

Checking energy versus pressure-amplitude reflection factors by hand prevents parameter mix-ups. Under a simplified real-valued specular wall model without transmission or phase shift, an energy absorption coefficient $\alpha=0.36$ retains $1-\alpha=0.64$ of incident acoustic energy, corresponding to a pressure reflection factor $\beta=\sqrt{0.64}=0.8$. Across two identical wall bounces, the pressure amplitude factor is $0.8^2=0.64$ while the energy factor is $0.64^2=0.4096$; one must never treat $0.36$ as an amplitude loss and square $1-0.36$ as a pressure gain.

In [Allen and Berkley's 1979 paper](https://users.umiacs.umd.edu/~ramanid/cmsc828d_audio/AllenBerkley79.pdf) (university-hosted copy, §§I.A–I.B, printed pp. 943–945), discrete image sources map directly to RIR dirac taps, and Eq. (9) explicitly relates energy absorption to pressure reflection. Our book uses the independent MIT-licensed `pyroomacoustics` implementation; the historical FORTRAN listing printed in the 1979 paper is neither `pyroomacoustics` source code nor redistributable under `pyroomacoustics`'s MIT license.

Real wall absorption varies with frequency, angle of incidence, and mounting conditions; limiting values `0` and `1` represent mathematical extremes rather than proving that any physical tile or glass pane has frequency-invariant ideal behavior. Version 0.10.0 recommends `materials=Material(...)`, whereas the deprecated `absorption` keyword follows legacy conventions that must be converted carefully when reproducing older scripts.

Complete simulation manifests should record source coordinates, microphone coordinates, sampling rate, source/sensor directivity, air absorption, maximum ISM order, and whether randomized image-source perturbation (`rand_ism`) is enabled. Because exact shoebox symmetry creates periodic flutter-echo artifacts, randomized ISM perturbs image coordinates to break coherent sweeping echoes—modifying the simulation prior without proving closer fidelity to an arbitrary physical room.

#### 2.2 Verifying Synthesized RIRs Step by Step

Recommended validation progresses from simple to complex: first verify direct-path arrival time and $1/r$ attenuation in an anechoic (`max_order=0`) setup; next verify single-wall image path length; only after adding higher-order reflections inspect energy decay curves, direct-to-reverberant ratio (DRR), and reflection-order convergence. At every step, verify time-zero alignment, fractional-delay FIR filter group delay (`delay=40` samples by default), built-in high-pass filtering, and tail truncation.

Exercise 16 in Appendix B executes our [six-position room SRP script](../../appendix_b/examples/room_srp_exercise.py) inside an isolated `pyroomacoustics` 0.10.0 environment, archiving [18 synthetic white-noise WAVs and full parameter manifests](../../appendix_b/room_audio/MANIFEST.json). Because v0.10.0 applies a default 10 Hz zero-phase high-pass filter independently to each generated RIR, zero-padding a short `max_order=0` RIR and subtracting it from a long full-order RIR leaks high-pass boundary transients into the putative "reverberant tail." Our script avoids this pitfall by constructing a fully absorptive reference room with identical filter settings and length, cross-checked against analytical single-image-source fractional-delay filtering of matching length.

Across the six source positions, $T_{20}$-extrapolated $T_{60}$ spans approximately 0.546–0.563 s (below the 0.6 s `inverse_sabine` design input), and SRP-PHAT azimuth errors span approximately 0.604°–2°. #### 2.4 Exponential Swept-Sine (ESS) and Band-Limited Deconvolution: pyfar

Farina's AES 108th Convention preprint *Simultaneous measurement of impulse response and distortion with a swept-sine technique* (2000, Preprint 5093, [author PDF](https://angelofarina.it/Public/Papers/134-AES00.PDF), §2, printed pp. 2–7) derives the exponential sine sweep, its amplitude-modulated matched inverse filter, and the temporal separation of linear impulse responses from causal/non-causal harmonic distortion orders after linear deconvolution. It addresses acoustic transfer measurement rather than claiming that playing any sweep automatically yields a clean room RIR or recovers arbitrary time-varying nonlinearities. `pyfar` is an independent general-purpose acoustics library whose regularized spectral inversion routines audited below do not constitute a complete reproduction of Farina's harmonic-separation measurement workflow.

### 3. STFT and Weighted Overlap-Add (WOLA) Reconstruction

Corresponding to main text §2.5. Our [`spectral.py`](../../ch02/core/spectral.py) fixes multichannel spectrograms to shape `M × F × L` (channels, frequency bins, frames). For real-valued signals of DFT size $N$, single-sided bin $0\le k\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ maps to physical frequency $f_k=k f_s/N$ in Hz, whereas upper bins $k>N/2$ of a full complex DFT map to negative frequencies $(k-N)f_s/N$; raw integer bin index $k$ must never be plugged directly into $e^{-j2\pi f\tau}$. Frame indices, sample indices, and wall-clock seconds must likewise be converted explicitly at module boundaries.

#### 3.1 Perfect Reconstruction Does Not Imply Multiplicative Transfer Equivalence

While the continuous or full-length discrete Fourier transform diagonalizes linear convolution, the STFT multiplies by a finite window before hopping across frames—so an exact LTI input–output relation in the STFT domain generally requires both cross-frame convolution and cross-band coupling. Writing each $(k,\ell)$ bin independently as $Y(k,\ell)\approx H(k)X(k,\ell)$ is the Multiplicative Transfer Function (MTF) approximation; a near-zero STFT–iSTFT round-trip error (`iSTFT(STFT(x)) = x`) does not imply that MTF holds.

In [Avargel and Cohen's 2007 paper](https://webee.technion.ac.il/Sites/People/IsraelCohen/Publications/TASL_May2007.pdf) (§II, Eqs. (7)–(11), printed p. 1307), an exact LTI system in the STFT domain decomposes into a sum of within-band and cross-band Convolutive Transfer Function (CTF) filters across frames. How many cross-band terms matter depends on analysis/synthesis window sidelobes, while cross-frame tap length grows with RIR length; adding more free CTF parameters also raises finite-sample estimation variance, so "more cross-band taps" does not unconditionally improve real-world performance.

A minimal counterexample is a pure delay exceeding the analysis window length: an impulse arriving in frame 0 produces zero input energy in frame 2, yet the delayed output arrives in frame 2—which no memoryless bin-wise multiplier $H(k)X(k,2)=0$ can generate. Always compute the exact time-domain linear convolution first, apply the identical STFT window, hop, and padding to obtain the reference spectrogram, and measure MTF approximation residuals against that true reference rather than generating ground truth from the MTF model under test.

#### 3.2 Synthesis Windows and Finite-Signal Endpoints

In `pyroomacoustics`, `transform/stft.py::compute_synthesis_window` divides the analysis window by the periodic sum of squared shifted analysis windows. While illustrating why a nonzero denominator matters, its circular shift summation on a single window block does not automatically guarantee boundary coverage at the first and last samples of a finite unpadded recording. [Pinned synthesis-window source](https://github.com/LCAV/pyroomacoustics/blob/0dd39f2614b7fc44b2cc63dbe7d60f4641068890/pyroomacoustics/transform/stft.py).

Exercise E02-03 demonstrates this endpoint trap using a zero-endpoint Hann window without boundary centering/padding: interior COLA/NOLA denominator positivity holds, yet sample 0 receives zero window weight across every frame and cannot be recovered. External `scipy.signal.stft` exposes explicit `boundary` and `padded` arguments (noting in SciPy 1.18.0 documentation, verified 2026-09-28, that `stft` is marked legacy in favor of `ShortTimeFFT`, requiring careful alignment of window scaling, time-axis origin, and boundary conventions during migration). [SciPy stft boundary docs](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.stft.html); [check_NOLA docs](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.check_NOLA.html).

Engineering regression suites should test impulses at $n=0$ and $n=N_{\mathrm{sig}}-1$, DC constants, and prime-length random sequences not divisible by hop size—verifying exact output length, algorithmic framing delay, and tail flushing, since steady-state interior sinusoids easily miss boundary bugs. Real-time streaming engines must additionally document samples consumed/emitted per callback, persistent overlap buffers, lookahead frames, and end-of-stream drain behavior; callback block size does not automatically equal total algorithmic latency.

Power spectral density (PSD) scaling requires its own independent check. Single-sided power spectra of real signals must fold conjugate-symmetric interior bins by a factor of 2 while counting DC ($k=0$) and even-$N$ Nyquist ($k=N/2$) bins once; magnitude spectra must not be scaled by power factors. Note that `scipy.signal.periodogram` defaults to `detrend='constant'` and `scaling='density'` ($\mathrm{unit}^2/\mathrm{Hz}$), meaning default detrending zeroes out any DC offset. [NumPy `rfft` normalization docs](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.fft.rfft.html); [SciPy periodogram docs](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.periodogram.html) (reviewed 2026-09-30). Exercise E02-08 verifies our own single-sided scaling directly via Parseval's theorem without claiming execution of SciPy in our base `.venv`.

#### 3.3 Modified Spectrograms Must Also Satisfy Cross-Frame Consistency

Satisfying NOLA (nonzero overlap-add squared-window denominator) and keeping DC/even-Nyquist imaginary parts zero at each frame ensures only that each individual frame represents a real sequence—yet overlapping frames of an arbitrarily modified spectrogram can still assign conflicting values to shared time-domain samples. Main text [Exercise E02-18](../../../../chapters_en/02_basics-signal-model.md#e02-18) demonstrates this conflict step by step using a 4-point rectangular window, hop size 2, and three centered frames.

Synthesizing a candidate spectrogram via WOLA and re-analyzing with the paired STFT projects onto the subspace of consistent spectrograms realizable by a single time-domain waveform. Whenever the analysis/synthesis pair achieves perfect reconstruction on valid waveforms, this `STFT(iSTFT(·))` map is idempotent; whether it is an *orthogonal* projection further requires specifying the Frobenius inner product and least-squares synthesis window. Crucially, under the full-DFT Euclidean metric on conjugate-symmetric spectra, interior single-sided `rfft` bins carry weight `2` while DC and Nyquist carry weight `1` (`[1, 2, 1]` for $N=4$), so unweighted `rfft` Euclidean distance is not the same norm.

Primary references are [Griffin & Lim (1984), §II, Eqs. (4)–(6), printed pp. 236–237](https://dub.ucsd.edu/CATbox/Reader/GriffinLimMSTFT.pdf), [SciPy `istft` Notes](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.istft.html), and [Wisdom et al. (2019), §3.1, Eq. (3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.08521). Our [minimal consistency core](../../ch02/core/stft_consistency.py) executes only the deterministic 3-frame control without running iterative Griffin–Lim phase reconstruction, neural separators, or SciPy wrappers; [Chapter 8, Eq. (8-31)](../../../../chapters_en/08_speech-separation.md#sec-u-110769710d) further contrasts single-stream STFT consistency against multi-source mixture consistency.

### 4. Batch, Masked, and Recursive Spatial Covariance Estimation

Corresponding to main text §2.5. In our [`covariance.py`](../../ch03/core/covariance.py), the estimator forms uncentered spatial second-order moments $\hat{\mathbf R} = \sum_t w_t \vec x_t \vec x_t^H / \sum_t w_t$. Zero-mean assumptions, sample-mean subtraction, and Bessel's $L-1$ denominator must be verified explicitly rather than assumed from the word `covariance` in a function name. A single snapshot outer product has rank at most 1, and an uncentered average of $L$ frames has rank at most $\min(M,L)$.

#### 4.1 Weight Normalization Adds Zero Statistical Information

Mask-weighted spatial covariance normalizes by $\sum_t w_t$, so scaling all positive weights by a common constant $c>0$ leaves $\hat{\mathbf R}$ invariant; our implementation pre-divides by $\max_t w_t$ so extreme scales such as $10^{-200}$ are not misclassified as empty support (verified across $10^{-200}, 1, 10^{200}$ in Exercise E02-06), while explicitly rejecting all-zero weights.

Keep "nonzero weight sum," "full matrix rank," and "statistically independent snapshots" strictly distinct: averaging 1,000 collinear snapshots still yields a rank-1 matrix, and heavily overlapped STFT frames contain fewer degrees of freedom than $L$ independent Gaussian draws. Production telemetry should log total weight mass, effective sample size $(\sum w_t)^2/\sum w_t^2$, eigenvalues/condition number, and active update intervals—not matrix dimensions alone.

#### 4.2 Verifying Forgetting Horizons and Numerical Positive Definiteness Separately

Exponentially weighted recursive estimation must track cumulative weight normalization when initialized from zero, lest early estimates suffer downward initialization bias (Exercise E02-05), while Exercise E02-07 converts the per-frame forgetting factor $\alpha$ into physical time constants in seconds. Note that when a VAD freezes covariance updates during speech, the frozen state does not decay with wall-clock time—and variable hop sizes invalidate fixed-hop time-constant formulas.

Oracle/empirical shrinkage blends $\hat{\mathbf R}$ toward the scaled identity $\mu\mathbf I$ with $\mu=\operatorname{tr}(\hat{\mathbf R})/M$, mapping each eigenvalue $\lambda_i \mapsto (1-\rho)\lambda_i + \rho\mu$. Only when $\hat{\mathbf R}\succeq\mathbf 0$, $\mu>0$, and $\rho\in(0,1]$ does shrinkage lift zero eigenvalues strictly above zero; an all-zero matrix ($\mu=0$) stays identically zero.

Here a user-supplied $\rho$ represents fixed regularization strength rather than Ledoit–Wolf optimal shrinkage estimation, whose real i.i.d. assumptions also require re-derivation for complex overlapped STFT snapshots. Above all, shrinkage and diagonal loading improve numerical conditioning without repairing target-speech leakage, swapped microphone channels, or corrupted noise-estimation intervals.

#### 4.3 Parameter Identifiability in Theoretical CRB Interfaces

Equation (2-14) in Chapter 2 gives the deterministic single-source Cramér–Rao Bound (CRB). In pinned `doatools`, `performance/crb.py::crb_det_farfield_1d` projects steering-vector derivatives onto the orthogonal complement $\mathbf P_{\mathbf A}^\perp = \mathbf I - \mathbf A(\mathbf A^H\mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf A^H$ before combining with the sample source second-moment matrix and noise variance. Geometrically, this orthogonal projection concentrates out unknown deterministic complex source waveforms (nuisance parameters), explaining why sensor coordinates enter relative to the array centroid. [Pinned CRB source](https://github.com/morriswmz/doatools.py/blob/9469db201e0418aef6b97583ef54b6fec2769502/doatools/performance/crb.py).

In this API, `sigma` denotes sensor noise **variance** $\sigma_n^2$ (not standard deviation), `P` is the $K\times K$ deterministic source sample second-moment matrix, and `n_snapshots` is the number of independent snapshots. Neighboring functions `crb_sto_farfield_1d` (unconstrained stochastic Gaussian sources) and `crb_stouc_farfield_1d` (uncorrelated stochastic sources) implement distinct statistical observation models whose bounds must not be interchanged, just as Stoica & Nehorai's 1990 comparative paper cited in `doatools` differs from their 1989 foundational paper cited in our main text.

Our Chapter 2 contract harness extracts the unmodified `SourcePlacement`, `FarField1DSourcePlacement` (`phase_delay_matrix`), `projm`, `reduce_output_matrix`, and `crb_det_farfield_1d` definitions, pairing them with a minimal 6-element ideal ULA adapter that maps phase delays and derivatives to complex steering vectors without importing full `ArrayDesign` or solver modules.

## Localization Algorithms and Search Acceleration

### 7. GCC-PHAT and Physical Lag Cropping

Corresponding to §4.2. Our `doa.py::gcc_phat` makes the positive-delay convention, zero-padded FFT size, physically admissible lag cropping, and three-point parabolic sub-sample interpolation explicit; in ODAS, `src/signal/xcorr.c` and `src/system/freq2xcorr.c` provide a block-processing C reference. Note that zero-padding to $N_1 + N_2 - 1$ guarantees exact equivalence to acyclic linear cross-correlation *only* for unweighted CC: once PHAT divides out cross-spectral magnitudes, the whitened spectrum no longer corresponds to a finite-support convolution, so changing FFT zero-padding length alters interpolated GCC-PHAT values. Furthermore, because PHAT equalizes spectral magnitude, low-energy bins exhibit erratic phase noise—making magnitude floor clamping and active passband selection integral parts of the algorithm.

Recommended verification feeds a 0.5 ms delayed wideband source followed by a pure sinusoid, dual sources, and digital silence—reporting primary peak, secondary peak, peak-to-sidelobe ratio, and maximum physically admissible lag $\tau_{\max}=d/c$. Parabolic sub-sample interpolation refines correlation peak location on bandlimited signals without creating identifiability out of single-tone periodic ambiguity. Fix microphone channel indices and the sign of $\tau_{12} = t_1 - t_2$ up front rather than flipping signs post hoc.

Classical CC, Roth, SCOT, and PHAT apply four distinct frequency weightings to the same cross-spectrum ([Knapp & Carter, 1976](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASSP.1976.1162830)): unweighted CC retains cross-spectral magnitude; Roth divides by one designated reference auto-spectrum (changing when channels are swapped); SCOT divides by $\sqrt{G_{11}(f)G_{22}(f)}$; and PHAT divides by $\lvert G_{12}(f)\rvert$. Notice a classic single-frame trap: if SCOT is evaluated on a single unsmoothed DFT frame where $\lvert X_1 X_2^*\rvert = \sqrt{\lvert X_1\rvert^2 \lvert X_2\rvert^2}$, SCOT degenerates numerically to PHAT—one must never conclude from a single-frame test that the two ensemble estimators are equivalent. Fair comparisons require multi-frame spectral averaging, matched regularization floors, and identical physical lag bounds. Our teaching module implements PHAT while documenting CC, Roth, and SCOT analytically in Chapter 4.

Production engines pre-compute static delay tables, prune redundant sensor pairs, enforce minimum inter-peak angular separation, and rebuild lookup tables whenever geometry or sampling rate changes.

**SRP score values are not directly interchangeable across libraries.** Our `srp_phat` accepts an `M × F × T` spectrogram, normalizes pairwise cross-spectra per frame, and averages real projections across frames, selected bins, and unordered pairs—returning a signed real score in $[-1, 1]$. By contrast, `pyroomacoustics`'s `SRP._process` normalizes each channel spectrum by its magnitude, accumulates cross-spectra across time, evaluates twice the upper-triangular real sum plus a direction-independent constant `T * M * F`, and divides by `T * F * P` (for $P = M(M-1)/2$ pairs). Only when all spectral magnitudes exceed both libraries' respective floors on identical bins, frames, geometry, and phase conventions does upstream `SRP` equal $2 \times \text{ours} + M/P$—sharing the exact $\arg\max$ direction while differing in baseline offset and peak-to-background ratio, and diverging on silent or low-energy bins where `T * M * F` injects synthetic constant self-power. ### 10. SVD-PHAT and Multi-Source Extensions

#### SMP-PHAT: Merging Redundant Baselines Instead of Truncating Matrices

An exact algebraic route to accelerating far-field SRP merges redundant baseline vectors: SMP-PHAT (*Steered response power by Merging Pairs with PHAse Transform*) exploits the fact that sensor pairs with identical baseline displacement $\vec p_i - \vec p_j$ experience identical far-field plane-wave TDOAs across *all* directions—allowing their PHAT cross-spectra to be summed in the frequency domain prior to a single shared inverse FFT and lag lookup (conjugating cross-spectra when baseline orientation is reversed). Crucially, pairs must share both equal length and parallel orientation; unequal-length collinear baselines cannot be merged. [Grondin et al. (2022 preprint), §3, Algorithms 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.14409v1).

Follow the upstream `smpphat` C implementation along `demo/ssl.c` → `src/system.c::scmphat_call` → `smp_construct` / `smp_call`, alongside unmerged `srp_construct` / `srp_call` in the same file and geometry/grid structs in `src/signal.c`. Recording channels must match coordinate indices, candidate directions are 3-D unit vectors, length and sound speed must share consistent units, and output scores are unnormalized correlation sums rather than calibrated probabilities. [Pinned system.c](https://github.com/FrancoisGrondin/smpphat/blob/6fd33e6eb3251078a4cd9793dde909e2500265cc/src/system.c).

Pinned commit `6fd33e6eb3251078a4cd9793dde909e2500265cc` imposes three concrete reproduction constraints:

1. `CMakeLists.txt` requires `pkg-config` and `fftw3f` while hardcoding `USE_SIMD`, `-msse3`, and `-ffast-math`; non-x86 hosts require explicit build adaptation, and x86 SIMD timing numbers cannot be extrapolated across architectures.
2. `wav_construct` reads a fixed C struct header accepting only canonical 16-bit PCM WAVs rather than general chunked RIFF containers.
3. `smp_construct` uses baseline-merging tolerance `1e-5` (whereas the paper text prints `1e-4`), applying that same scalar threshold to both baseline-length differences and normalized dot-product residuals—meaning rescaling coordinates from meters to millimeters alters pair grouping.

On 2026-09-23 we compiled and executed unmodified `system.c` and `signal.c` directly on macOS `arm64` using Apple clang 21 and static single-precision FFTW 3.3.10 (bypassing upstream's `-msse3` CMake wrapper without modifying a single line of C source). Importantly, SRP and SMP objects were run sequentially in isolated "construct → call → copy → destroy" lifecycles because upstream destructors call global `fftwf_cleanup()`, which invalidates any live FFTW plans held by coexisting instances ([FFTW plan lifecycle docs](https://fftw.org/fftw3_doc/Using-Plans.html)).

Our deterministic test uses a 4-microphone planar diamond of radius 0.032 m at $f_s=16$ kHz, $c=343$ m/s, 64-point base spectrum with $4\times$ zero-padding interpolation, scanning 24 horizontal unit vectors in 15° steps ($0^\circ$ along $+y$, rotating positively toward $+x$, true plane wave at $45^\circ$; DC set to `1`, positive bins 1–31 set to analytic unit-magnitude PHAT cross-spectra, Nyquist bin 32 set to `0`). Full directional arrays and SHA-256 digests are archived in [`smpphat_reference.json`](../../ch04/reports/smpphat_reference.json).

On the exact diamond, the 6 sensor pairs merge into 4 baseline groups; perturbing sensor 4 by 0.5 mm along $y$ splits them back into 6 unmerged groups. While our independent signed-lag lookup and direct DFT reference confirm that exact mathematical SRP and SMP scores agree within $1.99\times10^{-13}$ on the symmetric diamond, the unmodified upstream C code exhibits a maximum SRP-vs-SMP discrepancy of **76.5567**, with maximum errors of **95.1649** (SRP) and **171.7215** (SMP) against the analytical baseline. Even though both C curves happen to peak at 45°, peak coincidence here masks massive score corruption.

Tracing `system.c` pinpoints the root cause at two lag-table index expressions where negative `roundf(...)` floats are cast to `unsigned int` *before* adding the center lag offset. In C99/C11, casting a negative out-of-range float to `unsigned int` invokes undefined behavior ([Clang floating-point conversion manual](https://clang.llvm.org/docs/UsersManual.html)): on `arm64` Apple clang, negative floats saturate to `0u`, clamping every negative TDOA lag onto the zero-lag center bin (e.g., mapping true index 3 onto center index 9 across 66 SRP table entries). Feeding upstream's corrupted unsigned indices into our independent DFT evaluator reproduces the upstream C outputs within $3.35\times10^{-5}$ (SRP) and $2.36\times10^{-5}$ (SMP), proving that unsigned float casting—not FFT scaling—causes the failure.

Even more instructively, on the 0.5 mm perturbed array where no pairs merge, upstream SRP and SMP produce *identical* numbers to each other while both deviate from true physics by **96.0504**—proving why cross-checking two implementations that share the same bug never substitutes for an independent mathematical reference. Accordingly, [`smpphat_reference.json`](../../ch04/reports/smpphat_reference.json) records `status = "failed_portability"` while leaving upstream files untouched.

**Isolated portability overlay experiment (executed 2026-09-24).** Our [portability overlay script](../../ch04/examples/reproduce_smpphat_portable_overlay.py) verifies the clean upstream working tree first, copies the C sources into `/tmp`, casts the two `roundf(...)` expressions to signed `int` before adding the unsigned center offset, and re-runs both geometries. Under this two-token signed-cast fix, 4-group merged SMP and 6-pair SRP both peak at 45° (candidate index 3), agree with our independent signed DFT baseline within $1.34\times10^{-5}$ / $1.77\times10^{-5}$, and agree with each other within $3.10\times10^{-5}$ ([archived overlay report](../../ch04/reports/smpphat_portable_overlay.json)). This isolated overlay diagnoses the exact C portability bug on synthetic far-field cross-spectra without rewriting upstream's `failed_portability` status or claiming reverberant audio accuracy or CPU benchmarks; remember also that near-field wavefront curvature invalidates baseline merging because displaced parallel pairs subtend unequal ranges.

Corresponding to §4.6. Our `esprit_ula` solves the least-squares (LS) subarray rotational invariance system, paired externally with `doatools`'s `estimation/esprit.py`. ### 18. FRIDA Continuous-Angle Finite-Rate-of-Innovation Recovery

Corresponding to §4.7. In `pyroomacoustics`, `doa/frida.py` and `doa/tools_fri_doa_plane.py` map cross-spectral visibilities onto uniform Fourier series coefficients via linear operators and annihilating filters (`max_four`, `max_ini`, `max_iter`, `G_iter`, `signal_type`; [Pan et al., ICASSP 2017](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICASSP.2017.7952744); [original Python 2.7 experiment repo](https://github.com/LCAV/FRIDA)).

Crucially, "arbitrary array geometry" in FRIDA carries strict model qualifiers: §2.1.1 (Eqs. (1)–(2)) of the [authors' preprint](https://dokmanic.ece.illinois.edu/assets/pdf/Pan2016tv.pdf) assumes **2-D coplanar sensors**, **far-field propagation**, and **mutually uncorrelated sources**; full 3-D spherical recovery is listed as future work in §4, and pinned `FRIDA._process` explicitly raises an error when `dim == 3` ([pinned frida.py](https://github.com/LCAV/pyroomacoustics/blob/0dd39f2614b7fc44b2cc63dbe7d60f4641068890/pyroomacoustics/doa/frida.py)). Furthermore, `signal_type='visibility'` extracts off-diagonal covariance entries (where $M(M-1)$ coarray visibilities enable resolving more uncorrelated sources than sensors), whereas `signal_type='raw'` phase-aligns and averages raw complex spectra across frames—a distinct statistical object to which coarray source-capacity claims do not transfer, nor does dropping diagonal self-powers cancel coherent cross-terms. Note also SP-FRIDA-01: `n_rot` defaults to `1` in `__init__` despite its docstring stating `10`. Neither FRIDA branch was executed at runtime in this round.

### 19. Group-Sparse Localization and Sparse Covariance Matching

Corresponding to §4.7. In `doatools` (`estimation/sparse.py`), `GroupSparseEstimator.estimate(Y, k, l)` fits multi-snapshot matrices `M × T` with shared directional row sparsity ($\ell_{2,1}$ mixed norm), whereas `SparseCovarianceMatching.estimate(R, k, l, ...)` fits stacked real/imaginary vectorized covariances against nonnegative directional source powers (appending $\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf I)$ when white noise variance is unknown, assuming uncorrelated sources). Both peak-picking wrappers still require target source count `k` (so neither API is "source-count-free"), and regularization parameter `l` changes meaning across penalized, $\ell_1$-constrained, and $\ell_2$-residual-constrained formulations ([author sparse API](https://morriswmz.github.io/doatools.py/references/doatools.estimation.sparse.html); [pinned sparse.py](https://github.com/morriswmz/doatools.py/blob/9469db201e0418aef6b97583ef54b6fec2769502/doatools/estimation/sparse.py)). Optional convex solvers (`cvxpy`) are not installed in `.venv`, so these two estimators remain statically audited.

For **L1-SVD** ([Malioutov, Çetin, & Willsky, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSP.2005.850882)), `preprocessing.py::l1_svd(Y, k)` simply returns the top-$k$ scaled left singular vectors (`M × k` compressed data matrix) without solving any $\ell_1$ program itself; passing that `M × k` matrix into `GroupSparseEstimator` with `n_snapshots = k` completes the pipeline, noting that compressed subspace dimension `k` is not raw statistical snapshot count $T$ and truncating singular values can discard weak sources ([pinned preprocessing.py](https://github.com/morriswmz/doatools.py/blob/9469db201e0418aef6b97583ef54b6fec2769502/doatools/estimation/preprocessing.py)).

**Atomic-norm** gridless methods ([Tang et al., §2, Eq. (2.2)](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~brecht/papers/12.Tang.EtAl.ctscs.pdf); [Yang & Xie, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSP.2015.2420541)) formulate continuous line-spectrum recovery via Toeplitz semidefinite programming—connecting to ULA DOA through uniform spatial-frequency mapping $u=\sin\theta$ under minimum wrap-around separation conditions rather than providing infinite resolution on arbitrary 3-D microphone geometries. Atomic-norm methods remain principle-indexed here without SDP execution.

#### Multi-Snapshot, Multi-Frequency Sparse Bayesian Learning (SBL)

Sparse Bayesian Learning estimates nonnegative directional source-power hyperparameters `gamma` via evidence maximization / EM-style fixed-point updates ([Gerstoft et al., IEEE SPL 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/LSP.2016.2598550); [pinned Python core `SBL_MF_Python/sbl.py`](https://github.com/gerstoft/SBL/blob/d4bba35e9b60907d3024473ba5a41046450baae0/SBL_MF_Python/sbl.py), paired with MATLAB `SBL_MF_matlab/SBL_v4.m`, `Beamforming_demo.m`, and `SBLSet.m`).

Note the axis transpose relative to our book's `M × F × T` convention: upstream Python `SBL(A, Y, options)` expects dictionary `A` of shape `M × G × F` and snapshots `Y` of shape `M × L × F` ($G$ grid directions, $L$ snapshots), returning posterior power vector `gamma` and a report struct rather than angle values directly. Moreover, noise-variance updates divide residual trace projection by `M - options.Nsource` and peak selection takes `Nsource` peaks—so this implementation requires specifying `Nsource < M` and assumes spatially white per-frequency noise $\sigma_f^2\mathbf I_M$.

On 2026-09-22 we executed the unmodified GPL-3.0 upstream `sbl.py` via our clean-room harness [`reproduce_sbl_reference.py`](../../ch04/examples/reproduce_sbl_reference.py) (Python 3.13.12, NumPy 2.5.3, `macOS arm64`), archiving full hyperparameter spectra, convergence traces, covariances, and SHA-256 digests in [`sbl_reference.json`](../../ch04/reports/sbl_reference.json) after verifying upstream `HEAD` and clean tracked status.

Our five-condition paired experiment uses a 4-microphone $\lambda/2$ ULA ($c=343$ m/s, $f=1000$ Hz, $d=0.1715$ m, scanning $-80^\circ\dots 80^\circ$ in $1^\circ$ steps with $a_m(\theta)=e^{+j\pi m\sin\theta}$, $\|\vec a(\theta)\|_2^2=4$, $L=200$ i.i.d. unit-power complex circular Gaussian source snapshots under seed `20260922`; baseline white noise variance `0.02` per sensor gives two-source-to-noise ratio $2/0.02 = 100 \equiv 20$ dB, `-10 dB` sets noise variance to `20`, and colored noise sets correlation $[0.9^{\lvert i-j\rvert}]$ at variance `0.02`; convergence threshold `1e-5`, max iterations `1000`, pruning ratio `1e-4`):

#### RobustSBL: M-Estimation Loss Selection Under Snapshot Outliers

Because a handful of impulsive outlier snapshots corrupt sample second-order moments, **RobustSBL** reweights each snapshot according to its Mahalanobis distance under the current scatter matrix—offering Gaussian (`SBL-G`), Student-$t$ (`SBL-T`), Huber (`SBL-H`), and Tyler (`SBL-Tyl`) M-estimation branches rather than naive waveform clipping ([Mecklenbräuker et al., *Signal Processing* 220, 109461, 2024, §§3–4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sigpro.2024.109461); [author preprint §§III-B–E](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.06213v2)).

### 20. Delay-and-Sum (DSB), Superdirective, and Diagonally Loaded MVDR

Corresponding to §§5.2–5.4. Our `beamforming.py` traces the progression from data-independent weights to sample-covariance MVDR, always reporting target complex response, White Noise Gain (WNG), and output noise power simultaneously while distinguishing trace-normalized relative loading $(\delta\cdot\operatorname{tr}(\hat{\mathbf R})/M)\mathbf I$ from unnormalized absolute loading $\epsilon\mathbf I$.

Exercise E05-02 proves analytically why **finite-INR MVDR does not place an exact spatial zero at the interferer**: on a 2-microphone $\lambda/2$ array with target at $0^\circ$, interferer at $30^\circ$, and unit spatially white sensor noise, setting linear INR to `1, 10, 100` yields interferer-direction responses of `-9.03, -23.84, -43.10` dB while placing actual pattern nulls at `44.82°, 32.03°, 30.21°` (verified analytically and numerically in `test_codes_spatial_round3.py`, alongside scale-safe DSB normalization across $10^{-200}\dots 10^{200}$).

Our [common-input beamformer benchmark](../../ch05/beamformer_common_input_demo.py) (§5.10 and `tests/test_codes_beamformer_common_input.py`) evaluates 4-microphone DSB, unloaded MVDR, relative-loaded MVDR ($\delta=1$), and LCMV on the identical 2 kHz analytical interference-plus-noise covariance when nominal target is $0^\circ$, true target is $10^\circ$, and interferer is $40^\circ$—demonstrating target self-nulling and diagonal-loading recovery on an exact deterministic covariance model.

**Scope of classic distant-ASR benchmarks.** Kumatani, McDonough, and Raj's tutorial *Microphone Array Processing for Distant Speech Recognition: From Close-Talking Microphones to Far-Field Sensors* (*IEEE SPM* 29(6), 127–140, Nov. 2012; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/MSP.2012.2205285); [CMU-hosted copy](https://course.ece.cmu.edu/~ece792/handouts/KumataniEtAl12.pdf), pp. 137–139, Tables 4–5) compares a 32-element, 4.2 cm rigid spherical array against a 64-element, 1.26 m linear array using replayed 44.1 kHz TIMIT speech at $T_{60}\approx 525$ ms across four speaker-adaptation passes at $28^\circ$ and $68^\circ$—results tied to those two radically different apertures rather than a circular-array ranking. Likewise, [Dolph (1946)](https://doi.org/10.1109/JRPROC.1946.225956) optimizes equiripple sidelobes vs. first-null beamwidth strictly for uniform broadside linear arrays, and [`scipy.signal.windows.chebwin`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.windows.chebwin.html) normalizes peak tap value to `1` rather than unit sum (requiring explicit $\vec w^H\vec a(\theta_0)=1$ rescaling, distinct from §26's spherical Dolph–Chebyshev expansion).

### 21. LCMV, Frost, and Generalized Sidelobe Canceller (GSC)

Corresponding to §§5.5–5.6. LCMV solves multiple linear response constraints $\mathbf C^H\vec w = \vec g$ in closed form; Frost projects stochastic gradient updates onto the constraint hyperplane across broadband time-domain taps ([Frost, 1972](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1972.8817), §II Eq. (16) and §III Eqs. (19)–(22), including cumulative numerical drift correction); and GSC decomposes constrained adaptation into a quiescent target-preserving branch minus a blocking-matrix adaptive noise-cancelling branch. Our `beamforming.py` and `gsc.py::ScalarGSCNLMS` provide closed-form LCMV, blocking-matrix verification, and stateful sample-by-sample complex NLMS with explicit adaptation freeze gates (`update_enabled`).

**Finite return values do not imply feasible linear constraints.** In our [pinned `pb_bss` audit](#beamformer-upstream-audit), passing two identical constraint columns `C = [[1, 1], [1, 1]]` with contradictory desired responses `g = [1, 0]` (an infeasible system) causes `get_lcmv_vector` to fall back to `lstsq` on the singular matrix—silently returning `[0.25, 0.25]` with actual response `C^H w = [0.5, 0.5]` and constraint residual `1/√2`. Meanwhile, sibling `get_lcmv_vector_souden` immediately raises `NotImplementedError` in pinned `pb_bss`. Always check `rank(C)` and post-solve $\|\mathbf C^H\vec w - \vec g\|_2$.

In **BTK2.0** (`btk20_src/unit_test/test_online_beamforming.py::online_beamforming` → `btk20_src/lib/pybeamformer.py::SubbandGSCLMSBeamformer` / `SubbandGSCRLSBeamformer` → C++ `btk20_src/beamformer/beamformer.{h,cc}`), stateful subband GSC updates operate in millimeter coordinates (`sound_speed = 343740.0` mm/s), target Python 2.7 / SWIG 3 / GSL / `libsndfile` (loading filterbank prototypes via `pickle`, which must never ingest untrusted binaries), output the quiescent branch for the first 128 frames while already adapting blocking weights whenever energy exceeds threshold (`beta=0.97`, `gamma=0.01`, norm cap, plus step-size halving every 4096 frames). [Pinned pybeamformer.py](https://github.com/kkumatani/distant_speech_recognition/blob/feff19ec8bcb770f6530fe280dc3ccafc2f5984a/btk20_src/lib/pybeamformer.py); [CMakeLists.txt](https://github.com/kkumatani/distant_speech_recognition/blob/feff19ec8bcb770f6530fe280dc3ccafc2f5984a/btk20_src/CMakeLists.txt); [online demo](https://github.com/kkumatani/distant_speech_recognition/blob/feff19ec8bcb770f6530fe280dc3ccafc2f5984a/btk20_src/unit_test/test_online_beamforming.py).

### 22. Worst-Case Robust Beamforming and WNG Constraints

Corresponding to §5.4.1. Worst-case robust MVDR ([Vorobyov, Gershman, & Luo, 2003](https://users.aalto.fi/~vorobys1/RobBeamformer.pdf), §III.A, Eqs. ### 37. TDOA Nonlinear Least Squares and Range Observability

Corresponding to §§4.4 and 4.7. Range-difference localization weights TDOA residuals by their shared-reference error covariance $\mathbf\Sigma_{\tau}$ (not independent identity weights when sensor 1 is shared) and linearizes via range-difference Jacobians. As source range $r$ grows relative to aperture $D$, radial sensitivity vanishes—so Gauss–Newton converging to a finite number does not prove range observability. Compare algebraic two-stage initialization ([Chan & Ho, 1994](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.301830)) against iterative Taylor-series linearization ([Foy, 1976](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAES.1976.308294)) without mislabeling generic `scipy.optimize.least_squares` calls as Chan–Ho's estimator.

#### 39.2 Direct Augmentation vs. Coarray Spatial Smoothing

Follow `WeightFunction1D` → `utils/math.py::vec` → [`estimation/coarray.py::CoarrayACMBuilder1D.transform`](https://github.com/morriswmz/doatools.py/blob/9469db201e0418aef6b97583ef54b6fec2769502/doatools/estimation/coarray.py), mapping an $M\times M$ physical covariance ($R_{ij}=\mathbb E[X_i X_j^*]$ at lag $\ell = p_i - p_j$) into an $M_v \times M_v$ virtual matrix ($M_v = L+1$) after averaging redundant pairs into $z = [r[-L],\dots,r[L]]^\top$:

- `method='da'` (**Direct Augmentation**): populates Toeplitz entries $T_{ij} = r[i-j]$. On finite snapshots, $\mathbf T$ remains Hermitian when $\hat{\mathbf R}$ is Hermitian, yet **$\mathbf T$ can exhibit negative eigenvalues** even when $\hat{\mathbf R} \succeq \mathbf 0$.
- `method='ss'` (**Coarray Spatial Smoothing**, default): averages outer products of all $M_v$ contiguous length-$M_v$ subvectors $\vec z_q$, forming $\mathbf R_{\mathrm{ss}} = M_v^{-1}\sum_q \vec z_q\vec z_q^H = M_v^{-1}\mathbf T\mathbf T^H \succeq \mathbf 0$.

Notice the algebraic consequence $\mathbf R_{\mathrm{ss}} = \mathbf T\mathbf T^H / M_v$: for Hermitian $\mathbf T$, coarray spatial smoothing squares every eigenvalue of $\mathbf T$ and divides by $M_v$—erasing negative eigenvalue signs and potentially reordering eigenvalue magnitudes, while changing physical units from $\mathrm{V}^2$ ($\mathbf T$, scaling as $\lvert b\rvert^2$) to $\mathrm{V}^4$ ($\mathbf R_{\mathrm{ss}}$, scaling as $\lvert b\rvert^4$). Thus $\mathbf R_{\mathrm{ss}}$ constructs a positive-semidefinite signal/noise subspace ([Pal & Vaidyanathan, 2010, §IV-A, pp. 4172–4174 & Caltech errata](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/e8ge0-xc746)) and must never be interpreted entrywise as $\mathrm{V}^2$ physical channel power.

**Fundamental unidentifiability of joint unknown DOAs and unanchored sensor phases.** On a ULA with spacing $d$, $\kappa = 2\pi f/c$, and $u_q = \sin\theta_q$, single-source model $x_{mqt} = g_m e^{j\kappa m d u_q} b_{qt}$ is *identically invariant* under shifting all directional sines $u_q \mapsto u_q + \delta$ while tilting channel phases $g_m \mapsto g_m e^{-j\kappa m d \delta}$ (preserving reference $g_0' = g_0$). Fixing reference channel 0, adding more unknown source scenes, or collecting more snapshots cannot break this continuous gauge freedom ([Tashev, ICME 2004](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2004/06/2004-ivantash-icme.pdf) on gain calibration; [Weiss & Friedlander, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01201215) on joint DOA/gain-phase identifiability).

Whereas Time-of-Arrival (TOA) self-calibration ([Kuang et al., ICASSP 2013](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/e90f122e-669e-44c8-8da8-41fae8645aae)) observes absolute transmitter-to-receiver distances, TDOA self-calibration ([Zhayida et al., 2016 preprint §§2, 5–6, 10](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.02392); GPL-3.0 [`StructureFromSound`](https://github.com/kalleastrom/StructureFromSound/tree/9b7db79a489d347bed9a38bd38224e65c273660a), [LICENSE.md](https://github.com/kalleastrom/StructureFromSound/blob/9b7db79a489d347bed9a38bd38224e65c273660a/LICENSE.md)) introduces unknown emission-time spatial offsets $o_j$ per acoustic event while still requiring **synchronized receiver clocks** and known sound speed. Following [`matlab/main.m`](https://github.com/kalleastrom/StructureFromSound/blob/9b7db79a489d347bed9a38bd38224e65c273660a/matlab/main.m) → `sfs_system_v1` → `main_gcctracking_rex` → [`tdoa/calcresandjac.m`](https://github.com/kalleastrom/StructureFromSound/blob/9b7db79a489d347bed9a38bd38224e65c273660a/matlab/tdoa/calcresandjac.m), residual $e_{ij} = \|\vec x_i - \vec y_j\| + o_j - D_{ij}$ operates in **meters** (with $o_j = -\|\vec x_1 - \vec y_j\|$ for reference mic 1, e.g., `(0,0,0)` and `(3,0,0)` m with source `(0,4,0)` m gives ranges `4, 5` m, $D = [0, 1]$ m, $o = -4$ m, residual `[0, 0]`, recoverable only up to rigid Euclidean transformation $\mathrm{E}(3)$).

### 47. Blind Analytic Normalization (BAN) for GEV

Corresponding to §5.9. `pb_bss`'s `blind_analytic_normalization` multiplies $\vec w$ by $\sqrt{\vec w^H\mathbf R_n^2\vec w} / \lvert\vec w^H\mathbf R_n\vec w\rvert$: for $\mathbf R_n=\mathbf I$, scaling `[1, 1]` by `1, 2, -1, j` yields `[1,1]/√2` (first two) and preserves `-1` and `j` phases (last two), giving target responses `√2, √2, -√2, -j√2` on $\vec a=[1,1]^\top$ (**unit weight norm $\neq$ unit target response**). Moreover, **same-named BAN functions differ across libraries**: in pinned [ESPnet `beamformer.py`](https://github.com/espnet/espnet/blob/be79590bb2ff26ffb01bc825c5f68cb9418b7f0d/espnet2/enh/layers/beamformer.py), `blind_analytic_normalization` returns the scalar *gain* (not the scaled vector) and divides by channel count squared $C^2$ (`1/4` vs. `1` for $C=2, \mathbf R_n=\mathbf I$).

### 48. Relative Transfer Function (RTF) Estimation and Souden vs. MERL MVDR

Corresponding to §§5.4 and 5.9 (`pb_bss::get_pca_vector`, `ESPnet::get_rtf`—which returns an unnormalized eigenvector before dividing by reference channel $b_r$). In `get_power_spectral_density_matrix` on `X[0] = I_2`, float mask `[1, 1]` gives `I_2 / 2`, integer mask raises in-place division `TypeError`, boolean mask raises `AttributeError` (`np.asfarray` removed in NumPy 2), negative mask `[2, -1]` produces indefinite `diag(2, -1)`, zero mask returns `0`, and tiny mask `[1e-300, 1e-300]` hits `eps=1e-10` denominator clamping to return `~1e-290 * I_2`.

**Souden MVDR preserves the reference microphone's target image, not unit gain on unnormalized $\vec b$.** Matching [Exercise E05-11](../../../../chapters_en/05_beamforming.md#e05-11) with $\vec b = [2, 1+j]^\top$, $\mathbf R_s = 3\vec b\vec b^H$, and $\mathbf R_n = \operatorname{diag}(2, 1)$ ($q = \vec b^H\mathbf R_n^{-1}\vec b = 4$), executed `get_mvdr_vector_souden` gives $\vec w_r = b_r^* \mathbf R_n^{-1}\vec b / q$ so $\vec w_r^H\vec b = b_r$ ($\vec w_r^H (\vec b/b_r) = 1$):

| Reference Channel `r` | Upstream `get_mvdr_vector_souden` Weights | Target Inner Product $\vec w_r^H\vec b$ | Implied Normalized RTF $\vec a_r$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | `[0.5, 0.5 + 0.5j]` | `2` | `b / 2` |
| `1` | `[0.25 - 0.25j, 0.5]` | `1 + j` | `b / (1 + j)` |

Testing complex reference `1` specifically verifies the $b_r^*$ conjugation factor, and automatic SNR reference selection on `Rs = diag(1, 4), Rn = I_2` properly picks channel `1` (`[0, 0.8]`). By contrast, sibling **`get_mvdr_vector_merl` has a genuine bug** ([`audit_beamformer_reference.py`](../../ch05/examples/audit_beamformer_reference.py) / [`beamformer_reference_audit.json`](../../ch05/reports/beamformer_reference_audit.json)): it sums candidate output powers into a single scalar before calling `argmax`, **always picking reference column `0`** (`(0.2, 0)` instead of optimal `(0, 0.8)` on `Rs = diag(1, 4), Rn = I_2`).

### 49. MWF, SDW-MWF, and Rank-1 Trace Simplifications

Corresponding to [§5.5.1](../../../../chapters_en/05_beamforming.md#sec-5-5-1), §5.7, and §5.9. General Speech-Distortion-Weighted Multichannel Wiener Filtering solves $(\mathbf R_s + \mu\mathbf R_n)\vec w = \mathbf R_s\vec e_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (`ESPnet::get_sdw_mwf_vector`; note `ESPnet::get_mwf_vector`'s parameter `psd_n` actually expects *mixture* covariance per its docstring). By contrast, `pb_bss::get_wmwf_vector` evaluates the **rank-1 Woodbury trace simplification** $\mathbf\Phi\vec e_{\mathrm{ref}} / (\mu + \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf\Phi))$ with $\mathbf\Phi = \mathbf R_n^{-1}\mathbf R_s$: on full-rank `Rs = diag(2, 1), Rn = I_2, mu = 1, ref = 0`, general MWF gives `[2/3, 0]` whereas `get_wmwf_vector` returns `[1/2, 0]`, agreeing (`[2/3, 0]`) only when `Rs = diag(2, 0)` is genuinely rank-1.

### 50. MCRA: Noise Updating Controlled by Local Spectral Minima

Corresponding to §5.7 ([Cohen & Berdugo, *IEEE SPL* 2002](https://israelcohen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/SPL_Jan2002.pdf), §§II–III). Minima-Controlled Recursive Averaging estimates speech-presence probability from the ratio of time–frequency smoothed power to tracked local minima before modulating recursive noise-PSD updates—requiring distinct time constants for spectral smoothing, minimum-search windows, presence smoothing, and noise adaptation.

### 51. IMCRA: Two-Stage Smoothing and Minimum Tracking

Corresponding to §5.7 ([Cohen, *IEEE TSAP* 2003](https://israelcohen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/SAP_Sep2003.pdf), §§II–V). Improved MCRA uses rough first-stage voice activity detection to exclude strong speech bins during second-stage frequency–time smoothing and minimum tracking, followed by bias compensation; retained as a primary-paper reference without bundling third-party unverified MATLAB clones.

### 52. OM-LSA: Log-Spectral Amplitude Gain Under Speech-Presence Uncertainty

Corresponding to §5.7 ([Cohen & Berdugo, 2001 / author software page](https://israelcohen.com/software/); [Habets & Cohen, IWAENC 2006, §§III–IV](https://webee.technion.ac.il/Sites/People/IsraelCohen/Publications/IWAENC2006_Habets.pdf)). Optimally Modified LSA combines speech-active LSA gain $G_H$ and speech-absent floor $G_{\min}$ **geometrically** as $G = G_H^p G_{\min}^{1-p}$: for $G_H=0.8, G_{\min}=0.1, p=0.5$, geometric gain is $\sqrt{0.08}\approx 0.283$ rather than arithmetic `0.45` (keeping standard fixed-$G_{\min}$ OM-LSA distinct from Habets & Cohen 2006's nonstationary-interference extension).

## 58. Direct-Path Evidence: DPD Screening vs. DP-RTF Estimation

A localizer observes the total reverberant microphone pressure field, not pre-isolated direct-path arrivals. High inter-channel coherence ($\lvert\gamma_{12}(f)\rvert \approx 1$) or a rank-1 noiseless narrowband covariance proves only that a single complex spatial vector dominates that bin—**when a specular wall reflection remains coherent with the direct sound, the combined vector $a_{\text{dir}} + g a_{\text{ref}}$ is still exact rank-1 with unit coherence yet points in a false direction.** Exercise E04-20 proves this spurious peak on a 2-microphone $\lambda/2$ array at 4 kHz, paired with [four synthetic reflection WAV controls](05_exercises_and_audio.md#sec-u-0697cc2c95).

### 58.1 Separating TF Bin Screening, Feature Estimation, and Manifold Matching

- **Direct-Path Dominance (DPD) test**: Screens time–frequency regions whose local spatial statistics resemble a single plane wave before feeding surviving TF bins into a downstream DOA estimator (without outputting clean speech or proving literal absence of reflections).
- **Direct-Path Relative Transfer Function (DP-RTF)**: Estimates the leading tap ratio $b_0(k)/a_0(k)$ of a multi-frame Convolutive Transfer Function (CTF) and matches that complex ratio against candidate steering vectors or HRIR manifolds.

| Method Family | Statistical Object Processed | Required Structural Assumptions | Scope in This Book |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency-smoothed singular-value-ratio DPD ([Nadiri & Rafaely, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASLP.2014.2337846)) | Local TF-averaged spherical-harmonic covariance | SH encoding, radial equalization, cross-bin manifold invariance, sufficient TF window | Reviewed via 2014 abstract + [Rafaely & Alhaiany 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sigpro.2017.08.010) ([author PDF §§II–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03688v1)); full DPD not implemented |
| Sound-field directivity DPD (Rafaely & Alhaiany, 2018) | Steered spherical-harmonic beam map | Array SH order $N$, directional peak-to-mean metric, threshold | Avoids cross-frequency SVD under spherical models |
| DP-RTF ([Li et al., *IEEE TASLP* 2016](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASLP.2016.2598319)) | Leading CTF tap ratio $b_0/a_0$ across frames | Cross-frame CTF validity, short window separating direct path from early reflections, stationary noise subtraction | Exercise E04-21 derives exact 3-row noiseless CTF cross-relation algebra |
| Naive single-bin coherence / rank-1 test | Unsmoothed single-bin microphone SCM | Single-frequency second-order moment | Exercise E04-20 proves vulnerability to coherent reflections |

### 58.2 Why Frequency Smoothing Requires Manifold Alignment and Noise Tracking

Because raw sensor steering vectors rotate with frequency, cross-frequency DPD smoothing requires either radial-equalized spherical harmonics or explicit wideband focusing ([2018 arbitrary-array focusing extension](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSEE.2018.8646090))—and any non-unitary focusing/radial filter colors spatially white sensor noise ([Exercise E04-17](../../../../chapters_en/04_doa-estimation.md#e04-17) vs. unitary [Exercise E04-18](../../../../chapters_en/04_doa-estimation.md#e04-18)).

### 58.3 Where DP-RTF's Leading Coefficient Ratio Comes From

In [Li et al. (2016), §§II–III, Eqs. (3), (7)–(12)](https://www.gipsa-lab.grenoble-inp.fr/~laurent.girin/papers/Li_et_al_TASLP_2016.pdf), approximating long RIRs by per-bin cross-frame CTFs $x_t = (a * s)_t$ and $y_t = (b * s)_t$ yields the commutative cross-relation $(a * y)_t = (b * x)_t$; dividing by $a_0 \neq 0$ produces a linear regression for $b_0/a_0$ (using ordinary non-conjugated multiplication and transpose—never Hermitian inner-product conjugation). Exercise E04-21 solves a $Q=2$ three-row exact system, contrasting leading direct-path ratio `1 + j` against full-path ratio `0.1 + 0.8j` at modulation frequency $\pi/2$. In noisy recordings, Li et al. form local inter-frame PSD/cross-PSD estimates and subtract nearby noise-segment PSDs—still requiring early reflections to arrive outside the first STFT window support, richly excited source spectra (a pure tone makes regression rows collinear), and $|a_0|$ bounded away from zero.

### 60.1 Batch-Axis Contract of `pb_bss::phase_correction` and Phase-Aware Normalization (PAN)

In pinned [`pb_bss` `beamformer.py:517–560`](https://github.com/fgnt/pb_bss/blob/10acc347fc9ea21e3d312806a0bd751d0d0af183/pb_bss/extraction/beamformer.py#L517-L560), `phase_correction` computes inner products across frequency axis `-2` yet calls cumulative product along hardcoded **`axis=0`**. Executed in [`upstream_beamformers_current.json`](../../ch05/reports/upstream_beamformers_current.json) on 3-bin sign pattern `(+, -, +)` across 2 channels: 2-D `(F, C)` input aligns signs to `(+, +, +)`, whereas 3-D single-batch `(1, F, C)` accumulates along the batch axis and leaves **`(+, +, -)`** (target response `(2, 2, -2)` on `[1, 1]`, invisible to squared-norm checks $\|\vec w\|_2^2 = 2$).

Furthermore, cross-frequency continuity does not fix absolute global phase ([Warsitz & Haeb-Umbach, 2006 §§2–3](https://groups.uni-paderborn.de/nt/pubs/2006/WaHa06-2.pdf); [2019 *IEEE TASLP* metadata](https://graz.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/eigenvector-based-speech-mask-estimation-for-multi-channel-speech)). **Phase-Aware Normalization (PAN)** ([Pfeifenberger et al., Interspeech 2017, §3.1, Eqs. (12)–(13)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2017/pfeifenberger17_interspeech.pdf)) anchors a nonzero GEV eigenvector $\vec w$ against a unit-norm phase-referenced target vector $\vec a$ ($\|\vec a\|_2=1$) and positive-definite $\mathbf R_n$:

$$\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{PAN}}&=\frac{\vec w^H\mathbf R_n\vec a}{\vec w^H\mathbf R_n\vec w},\\
\vec w_{\mathrm{PAN}}&=C_{\mathrm{PAN}}\vec w.
\end{aligned}$$

Whenever $\vec w \propto \mathbf R_n^{-1}\vec a$, $\vec w_{\mathrm{PAN}}$ recovers the exact MVDR response $\vec w_{\mathrm{PAN}}^H\vec a = 1$: e.g., for $\mathbf R_n = \mathbf I$, $\vec a = [1, 1]^\top/\sqrt{2}$, and $\vec w = j\vec a$, $C_{\mathrm{PAN}} = -j$ yields $\vec w_{\mathrm{PAN}} = \vec a$ ($\vec w_{\mathrm{PAN}}^H\vec a = 1$), whereas unit-norm BAN leaves $j\vec a$ (response $-j$). Note also that Pfeifenberger et al.'s Eq. (16) uses a centered 250 ms covariance window (~125 ms lookahead, non-causal).

### 61.1 Gaussian Normalization Factors and Dropout Protocol in IMM

Teaching kernel [`imm_teaching.py`](../../ch09/core/imm_teaching.py) pairs low/high process-variance scalar random-walk models ($Q_0=0, Q_1=4$, $R=1$, initial `mean=0, var=1`, `mu=[0.8, 0.2]`, Markov `[[0.9, 0.1], [0.2, 0.8]]`): when $z=3$ arrives (`cbar=[0.76, 0.24]`, innovation variances $S_0=2, S_1=6$), full Gaussian densities include prefactor $(2\pi S_j)^{-1/2}$ (`0.0297325723` vs. `0.0769331614`, so at zero innovation $S_0=2$ has $\sqrt{3}\times$ higher likelihood than $S_1=6$), giving posterior `mu ≈ [0.5503254309, 0.4496745691]`, fused `mean ≈ 1.9496745691`, `var ≈ 0.8973588741`; across two subsequent dropouts (`z=None`), zero false likelihoods are injected—propagating strictly via Markov priors so fused mean stays constant while variance grows to `2.5564476676` and `4.1178098232`.

## 62. Extension Topic III: Spatial Sound Field Capture, Parametric Coding, and Wearable Arrays

Earbuds, hearing aids, and smart glasses contend simultaneously with irregular sub-centimeter apertures, rigid-head scattering, occluded ear-canal resonances, and turbulent boundary-layer wind noise—requiring a sharp distinction between **parametric spatial audio reproduction** and **own-voice speech enhancement**. Four pure-NumPy teaching cores separate these contracts cleanly without misrepresenting synthetic two-tone WAVs as in-ear or wind-tunnel recordings:

### 62.1 Four Locally Runnable Numerical Cores and Interpretation Boundaries

- [`spatial_parametric.py`](../../ch14/core/spatial_parametric.py): Recomputes tetrahedral A-format to First-Order Ambisonics (FOA ACN/SN3D and FuMa) encoding, open-sphere $j_n(kr)$ radial-null Tikhonov regularization, $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ Wigner-real rotation matrices, and Directional Audio Coding (DirAC) active intensity / diffuseness decomposition.

- [`wearable_fusion.py`](../../ch14/core/wearable_fusion.py): Models Corcos turbulent boundary-layer wind-noise spatial coherence, split-band cross-modal MVDR/MMSE fusion of external air-conduction and occluded in-ear microphones, and TWS hear-through processing-delay comb filtering.
- [`ambisonic_sofa_rendering.py`](../../ch14/core/ambisonic_sofa_rendering.py): Verifies FOA max-$\vec r_E$ order-weighted loudspeaker decoding, AES69/SOFA `SimpleFreeFieldHRIR` coordinate transformations, and head-tracked motion-to-sound latency budgets.
