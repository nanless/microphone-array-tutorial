1. Define Signal Interfaces and Target Problems First

Acoustic Echo Cancellation (AEC) exploits a known loudspeaker playback reference to estimate and subtract the resulting acoustic echo; Weighted Prediction Error (WPE) uses delayed history of the microphone signals themselves to predict and subtract late reverberation; Blind Source Separation (BSS) relies on statistical independence, spectral low-rank structure, or spatial covariance models to separate multiple unknown sources; and supervised neural separation/extraction further requires clean training targets or target-speaker conditioning clues. Lumping all of these distinct modules under the generic label "noise reduction" obscures their fundamentally different input contracts and acceptance criteria.

Task Required Inputs Mandatory Persistent State or Conditions Minimal Acceptance Targets
AEC Synchronized microphone and playback reference Reference ring buffer, adaptive filter taps, bulk delay, step-size/freeze control Far-end single-talk cancellation, double-talk near-end preservation, echo-path change
WPE Phase-preserving single- or multi-channel STFT Delayed regressor history, prediction taps, time-varying power & weighted covariance Late-tail suppression, early-reflection/direct-path preservation, startup & prolonged silence
BSS Multi-channel mixture and declared source count Demixing matrices or source SCMs, projection-back scale, cross-frequency permutation alignment Separation gain, scale restoration, local optima sensitivity, rank-deficient mixtures
GSS Multi-channel recording and speaker activity diarization Activity masks, spatial cACGMM posteriors, speech/noise SCMs, reference mic Diarization errors, context padding, cross-frequency cluster consistency
Neural Separation / TSE Mixture audio, plus enrollment clue when conditioned Checkpoint weights, utterance/running normalization, temporal context, output slots Out-of-domain mixtures, missing sources, target over-suppression, strict causality
CSS Long unsegmented recording and chunking schedule Overlapped windows, cross-chunk permutation stitching, fixed output stream count Speaker turn-taking, silence gaps, duplicate leakage across slots, long-run RAM

Even array axis ordering can never be guessed from variable names alone: whereas general modules in this repository use (channels, freq_bins, frames), our teaching WPE and mask-based SCM kernels use (freq_bins, channels, frames), and ESPnet's DNN_WPE expects (batch, frames, channels, freq_bins). Always annotate axis permutations explicitly and test them on asymmetric two-channel signals rather than relying on coincidental shape compatibility.

2. AEC: From Adaptive Filtering to Complete Audio Processing Pipelines

A01 LMS, NLMS, and Leaky Updates

Normalized Least Mean Squares (NLMS) normalizes each gradient step by the instantaneous energy of the reference regressor vector, stabilizing adaptation across varying playback levels. Leaky NLMS additionally shrinks filter weights slightly toward zero on every step—bounding weight drift in poorly excited frequency bands at the cost of a small steady-state bias. Both require that the reference tap genuinely capture the loudspeaker signal generating the acoustic echo. Derivations and runnable baseline code appear in §6.1.2 and nlms() in aec.py.

Recommended code-reading trace: reference delay line -> echo estimate -> a priori error -> energy normalization denominator -> freeze gate -> tap update. A minimal verification experiment feeds a known short FIR echo path: verify weight convergence under near-end silence first, then inject near-end speech mid-utterance while freezing updates. For a failure control, shift pure bulk delay beyond the FIR filter length $L$—confirming that running more iterations can never recover taps outside the model support. When sweeping leakage factors, report parameter bias alongside output residual power.

Executed Exercise E06-11 cleanly separates statistical expectation bias from finite-sample online trajectory drift: on a deterministic 4-sample sequence where reference and near-end signals are strictly orthogonal, the exact batch least-squares tap is 0.8, yet four online NLMS steps initialized right at 0.8 drift to 0.89375 (whereas setting the near-end signal to $0.25\times$ the reference shifts the batch LS solution itself to 1.05). A single finite-sample trajectory endpoint must never be misdiagnosed as asymptotic estimator bias.

A02 FDAF, Multi-Tap Frequency-Domain NLMS, MDF/PBFDAF, and Overlap-Save

Single-block Frequency-Domain Adaptive Filtering (FDAF) replaces long time-domain convolution with FFT multiplication, while Multi-Delay Filtering (MDF) / Partitioned-Block FDAF (PBFDAF) splits a long echo tail into $P$ shorter partitions so algorithmic block latency stays small while covering long room tails. Fixing a single DFT bin, whenever the $P$ taps of "frequency-domain multi-tap NLMS" correspond to the current and $P-1$ past reference blocks and share the exact error spectrum and normalization denominator of Eq. (6-5), the two updates are component-wise identical rather than distinct algorithms—though identical terminology across papers never guarantees identical step-size control (Páez Borrallo & García Otero, 1992 is retained as a historical citation without inferring step-size heuristics from its abstract alone; constrained overlap-save derivations below follow Shynk's full text).

Never treat "multi-tap frequency-domain NLMS" as an unambiguous specification without checking three distinct tap domains: (1) sample-wise time-domain NLMS with $L$ sample delays; (2) partitioned overlap-save PBFDAF holding $P$ reference-block spectra per bin to represent an exact $L$-tap linear FIR; and (3) per-bin cross-frame filtering in a windowed STFT filterbank. Only (2) matches this book's PBFDAF baseline block-for-block when block size, history, valid-region error windowing, power smoothing, step size, time-domain gradient projection, and freeze scheduling all match; by contrast, windowed STFT subband filtering omits cross-band aliasing terms induced by sample-level FIR paths even under perfect-reconstruction analysis/synthesis windows—as derived originally in Avargel & Cohen (2007), §II Eqs. (7)–(11) and illustrated in Chapter 6 via a 2-point DFT unit-sample delay counterexample.

Denoting the $2N$-point DFT matrix by $\mathbf{F}$ and the valid-half selection matrix by $\mathbf{S}$, the exact overlap-save error spectrum is $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{F}\mathbf{S}^\top(\vec{d} - \mathbf{S}\mathbf{F}^{-1}\mathbf{Q})$—never unwindowed $\mathbf{D} - \mathbf{Q}$. While constrained FFT correlation without per-bin power normalization computes the exact block-LMS gradient on valid FIR taps (still distinct from sample-by-sample in-block weight updates), per-bin preconditioning by $\sum_p |X_p|^2 + \delta$ in Eq. (6-5) alters the time-domain gradient direction and re-couples DFT bins upon time-domain zero-padding projection—nor does that scalar sum invert the full $P \times P$ cross-partition reference correlation matrix of exact block RLS; see Shynk (1992), §IV, Fig. 5 & Eq. (30), Schneider & Kellermann (2016), §§3–4, Soo & Pang (1990), pinned SpeexDSP libspeexdsp/mdf.c, and Worked Example 6-4.

When reading speex_echo_state_init_mc() and speex_echo_cancellation(), trace partition count $P$, reference spectrum ring history, overlap-save output slicing, zero-padded error FFT, and time-domain gradient projection—checking block size, FFT scaling, initialization, instantaneous vs. smoothed bin power, regularization floor, DTD/leakage step control, and projection cadence item-by-item before comparing two PBFDAF implementations (and remembering that Worked Example 6-2's complexity speedup compares against sample-domain time NLMS, not a renamed frequency-domain variant).

Minimal experiments should freeze adaptation first to verify bit-accurate sample-domain linear convolution equivalence across partitions, then enable adaptation and inject two distinct faults: (a) omitting valid-half output slicing (which immediately corrupts linear convolution with circular wraparound), and (b) disabling time-domain gradient projection (unconstrained MDF/FDAF, a legitimate algorithmic variant that alters weight trajectories without omitting valid-output slicing; cf. Avargel & Cohen, 2007 §II).

This repository provides the self-contained partitioned PBFDAF kernel, two-block hand-check & system-identification demo, and unit tests: at $N = P = 2$, block 1's unconstrained partition-0 candidate exhibits a wrap-around 3rd time tap of $1/3$ that projection zeros out while partition 1 retains its valid 1st tap of $1/3$, alongside narrowband sine vs. broadband white-noise held-out identification controls proving that driving training residual to zero on a pure tone fails to identify the broadband room path (note also that Worked Example 6-2's 224 / 736 complex-op counts tally core FFTs and complex multiplies while excluding $|X|^2$ recomputations—at least +32 ops/sample—and Python diagnostic IFFTs).

A03 AUMDF, Partition Constraint Scheduling, and Proportional Adaptation

Comments in SpeexDSP's mdf.c specify Alternately Updated MDF (AUMDF), which rotates time-domain gradient-projection FFT/IFFT pairs across partitions to halve transform cost while applying proportional adaptation weights across partitions. Because proportional allocation steers larger steps toward dominant early partitions, fast convergence on sparse impulsive paths never proves equal convergence speed on diffuse reverberant tails.

While reading proportional adaptation rate and MDF / AUMDF blocks in mdf.c, distinguish "whether a partition undergoes time-domain projection on this frame" from "whether that partition receives a frequency-domain gradient update". Compare equal-length, equal-energy sparse vs. dense FIR paths and test abrupt shifts in the dominant reflection lag—without mislabeling Speex's partition-level proportional weighting as classic sample-wise PNLMS/IPNLMS.

A04 RLS, Kalman, FDKF, and Partitioned State-Space AEC

Keep estimation objectives distinct. Real time-domain Recursive Least Squares (RLS) in Eqs. (6-16)–(6-18) propagates the inverse exponentially weighted reference correlation matrix via the Woodbury / matrix inversion lemma to solve regularized weighted least squares exactly—requiring finite initialization $\mathbf{P}_{-1} = \delta^{-1}\mathbf{I}$ to match the explicit penalty $\lambda^{n+1}\delta\|\vec{w} - \vec{w}_{-1}\|^2$ on early samples, as verified in aec_rls.py, aec_rls_demo.py, and test_codes_aec_rls.py.

Standard $L$-tap RLS stores an $L \times L$ inverse covariance state at $O(L^2)$ operations per sample, diverges under prolonged zero reference when $\lambda \lt 1$ divides $\mathbf{P}$ repeatedly without excitation, and absorbs near-end speech into filter taps during unfrozen double-talk (while square-root, QR, and Householder RLS variants improve conditioning at distinct operation counts; note MathWorks dsp.RLSFilter docs document RLS variants while MathWorks' AEC tutorial actually uses FDAF). Note also that our teaching aec_rls.py runs an explicit $O(L^3)$ Cholesky positive-definiteness check on every accepted sample for numerical safety—so its Python runtime must never represent optimized $O(L^2)$ C RLS, while aec_rls_kalman_comparison.py logs true prior echo prediction error separately from double-talk total residual.

Fast RLS is distinct from standard frequency-domain NLMS. Whereas PBFDAF normalizes by scalar bin power, Schneider & Kellermann's GFDAF analysis (2016, §§3–4) connects generalized frequency-domain algorithms to approximate block RLS, and structured fast-RLS families—such as Cioffi & Kailath's Fast Transversal Filters (1984) and Slock & Maouche's FFT/displacement-rank RLS (1994)—carry their own rescue/stability preconditions.

Kalman AEC introduces an explicit state-space prior. Eqs. (6-20)–(6-22) model the time-varying acoustic path as a latent state with process covariance $\mathbf{Q}$ (path drift uncertainty), measurement variance $\Psi$ (near-end speech + background noise power), and state-error covariance $\mathbf{P}$ (distinct from RLS's inverse regressor correlation, coinciding algebraically only under $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{I}, \Psi=1, \mathbf{P}_n^- = \mathbf{P}_{n-1}/\lambda$, i.e., state-dependent $\mathbf{Q}_n = (\lambda^{-1}-1)\mathbf{P}_{n-1}$). Crucially, a spike in innovation power alone cannot distinguish double-talk (which demands raising $\Psi$ to slow adaptation) from abrupt echo-path change (which demands raising $\mathbf{Q}$ to speed re-convergence). Two standalone runnable demos verify exact rational arithmetic: single-bin complex scalar Kalman (tests: $K = 3/7$, posterior $22/35$, variance $3/35$; $\Psi: 0.4 \to 4 \implies K = 3/16$; $X = i \implies K = -i/2$) and 2-tap real matrix Joseph-form Kalman (tests: $\mathbf{P}^- = \operatorname{diag}(1, 4), \vec{x} = [1, 1]^\top, \Psi = 1 \implies \vec{K} = [1/6, 2/3]^\top$ with off-diagonal posterior $-2/3$, whereas forcing diagonal $\mathbf{P}$ distorts step-2 innovation variance from $9/2$ to $19/6$).

From full-matrix Kalman to FDKF / PBFDKF. Enzner & Vary (Signal Processing 2006, §§2–4) (author note) formulate state-space FDAF as a diagonalized frequency-domain Kalman approximation unifying linear subtraction and uncertainty-driven post-filtering, extended to partitioned blocks in Kuech, Mabande & Enzner (ICASSP 2014) and multi-channel/multi-reference covariance block structures (VD-PBFDKF vs. cross-speaker SD-PBFDKF, both dropping cross-partition blocks) in Zhu et al. (Interspeech 2021, §§2–3, Figs. 4–5)—always specify whether "diagonal" means diagonal across bins, partitions, speaker channels, or scalar entries.

A05 DTD: Binary Freeze Decisions vs. Continuous Step-Size Control

Double-Talk Detection (DTD) protects near-end speech from corrupting adaptive echo-path weights rather than synthesizing an echo replica directly. Whereas Geigel level ratios, cross-correlation, and coherence detectors output binary freeze gates, pinned SpeexDSP mdf.c explicitly eschews binary DTD in favor of continuous leakage-based learning-rate modulation (Valin, IEEE TASLP 2007), and WebRTC AEC3's subtractor.cc computes separate spectral update gains for its refined and coarse filters—neither can be reduced to a binary if (dtd) freeze flag.

Executed: 4-state synthetic DTD confusion matrix and abrupt path-change counterexample. Stopping far-end playback does not immediately silence room echo tails. When the current render frame goes silent while microphone energy persists, reverberant room decay from earlier render frames may still dominate. 3. WPE and Joint Convolutive Filtering

W01 Offline WPE and MIMO-WPE

Variance-normalized delayed linear prediction uses delayed multi-channel STFT history to predict and subtract current-frame late reverberation while time-varying inverse-power weights prevent loud speech onsets from dominating the regression. In multi-channel MIMO-WPE, the per-bin regressor dimension equals $\text{channels} \times \text{taps}$, becoming ill-conditioned under insufficient frames or collinear channels. Our teaching baseline lives in dereverberation.py, paired with upstream nara_wpe wpe.py (build_y_tilde(), wpe_v6(), etc.; Yoshioka & Nakatani, IEEE TASL 2012).

Trace lag ordering, prediction delay $\Delta$, "valid" vs. "full" covariance support, complex conjugation, and power floors—verifying single-tap closed-form normal equations first, then duplicating a mono channel into two identical channels, and injecting ultra-short clips, silent bins, and singular SCMs.

Executed locally on macOS arm64 (Python 3.13.12, NumPy 2.5.3, complex128) on 2026-09-22 and re-verified on 2026-09-23, compare_wpe_reference.py compares unmodified nara_wpe 0.0.11 wpe_v6() against offline_wpe() (seed=20260922, shape (4, 2, 192), 2 taps, delay=3, statistics_mode="valid", zero power smoothing, zero diagonal loading; 2-mic, 1-mic, channel-swapped, and scaled inputs across 1 and 3 iterations): excluding the first 4 startup frames (bypassed in our baseline vs. zero-padded in upstream), max absolute complex difference is 3.17e-15 and max relative $L_2$ difference is 5.76e-16.

Meanwhile, Exercise E07-04 proves offline non-causality on two 4-frame sequences sharing an identical prefix yet differing on the final frame—shifting frame-1 output from 0 to -1/9 because batch covariance pools future frames—and Exercise E07-06 derives from spherical Gaussian NLL ($M\log\lambda$) why shared variance equals the cross-channel mean squared magnitude ([1+j, 2] -> 3, doubling channel 1 amplitude $\to 9$; cf. diagonal vs. scaled-identity models in §§V-A/B and tail-energy caveats in §VI of Yoshioka & Nakatani 2012 full text).

Pinned 0.0.11 statistical conventions. build_y_tilde() stacks taps outer and channels inner; statistics_mode="valid" accumulates from delay + taps - 1 while predicting all frames; default wpe aliases wpe_v7, _stable_solve() falls back to lstsq without relative trace loading, and get_power_inverse() floors power at 1e-10 * max(power) across the entire passed array—meaning batching all bins in wpe_v6/v7 vs. looping bin-by-bin in wpe_v8 yields different weights on high-dynamic-range spectra (verified concretely in W11).

W02 Frame-Recursive Online WPE

Critical 0.0.11 lag-index shift across three same-named delay APIs. On 1-bin, 1-mic, 1-tap sequence [10, 20, 30, 40] at 0-based frame $t = 3$ (X_3 = 40) with delay = 1 and unit tap 1: - Offline build_y_tilde() taps $X_{t-1} = 30 \implies \text{residual } 10$; - Stateless online_wpe_step() taps $X_{t-2} = 20 \implies \text{residual } 20$; - Stateful OnlineWPE.step_frame() reads its ring buffer before inserting $X_t$, tapping $X_{t-3} = 10 \implies \text{residual } 30$.
